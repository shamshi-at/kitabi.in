Life and work

Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai was an Indian novelist and short-story writer of Malayalam literature. He began as a social realist documenting caste and labour in the Kuttanad region, and is best known for Chemmeen (1956), a novel of the fishing communities of the Kerala coast.

His later work turned to the chronicle: Kayar follows a single village across four generations and the land reforms that remade it. He received the Jnanpith Award in 1984.

No “Born — unknown”. No “Died — N/A”. The rows simply aren't there.

The rule this page is built around: facts about real people are never generated. Not as a fallback, not “to fill the gaps”, not with a disclaimer. A hallucinated death date or award about a living writer is defamatory in the ordinary sense of the word — and this catalogue is full of living writers, several of whom will eventually verify their own pages through the author-claim flow that already exists. An LLM may shorten a fetched paragraph; it may not be the source of one.

M6

Mobile — and the one ordering decision that matters

Most people who reach an author page arrive on a phone, from a search result, wanting one fact. So on mobile the infobox comes before the prose — the reverse of the desktop reading order, where it sits in a rail alongside. A reader who came for "when did he die" should not scroll past three paragraphs to find it.

Infobox above prose. On desktop the infobox is a rail the eye takes in alongside the lead; on a phone there is no alongside, only before and after. The question that brought the reader here is almost always a fact, not a paragraph — so the facts go first and the prose is truncated to a more link.
Three covers, not six. A phone row of six covers is six unreadable thumbnails. Three at a legible size plus an honest "All 34 →" is a better answer than a wall.
One app band, at the bottom. Never an interstitial over the content — Google penalises it and readers hate it. The same rule the book page already follows (web-mockups W13).
The attribution survives the squeeze. The CC BY-SA credit under a truncated Wikipedia paragraph is the one element that must never be dropped for space: it is a licence obligation, not a design flourish.

What “one stop for all lookups” can honestly mean. For a book, author, publisher, translator or series in fourteen Indian languages, this can be the best page on the internet — because for most of that catalogue, nothing good exists. It does not mean beating Wikipedia on Shakespeare: we hold 18 Shakespeare titles and that page will never win, nor should it try. For authors the world already documents, the honest play is to be the best page about their books in Indian languages and link out for the rest.

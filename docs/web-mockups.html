Why the language grid sits so high. It is not decoration — it is the crawl architecture. Home links all 14 language hubs; each hub links its books; therefore every indexable page is ≤3 clicks from the root. Google's crawl budget for a new domain is small, and a flat, shallow graph is the cheapest way to spend it well.

The book page — the flagship

Everything else on this site exists to send people here. The page answers four questions in the order they actually arrive: what is it → what do people think → what's it about → what do I do about it. Then, below the fold, in this order because it's the order the next questions arrive: read it in another language → which printing → what readers said → what else by this author → what's like it → the facts.

Note where the translation module sits: directly under the fold, above editions and above reviews. It is the one thing on this page that Goodreads, StoryGraph and Amazon structurally cannot show, so it gets the best position on the page that isn't the title.

One page, one API call Everything on this frame — work, editions, authors, translations, reviews, related books, breadcrumbs — arrives in a single GET /public/book/chemmeen. Four sequential round trips from the edge to Singapore would be ~1.2 s of TTFB on their own. Budget met: 39 ms warm, 11.4 KB HTML.
Outbound links: 41 Author, translators, publisher ×4, series, 5 genres, language hub, form hub, 2 translations, 12 related books, 4 edition anchors. A book page is the densest crawl node on the site — that is the whole reason the "more by / also shelved" rows exist.
Structured data Book with a workExample per edition, Person per author, Review per public review, BreadcrumbList, and AggregateRating — only because a real rating exists The existing _og.js already builds this object correctly; it gets lifted, not rewritten.
Never emit AggregateRating with a zero or invented value. Google issues manual actions for fabricated review markup, and Kitabi's aggregate_rating is legitimately null on most works today. When there's no rating, the block and the markup both disappear — the page simply doesn't have that section.

Genre hub — and the faceted browse behind it

One template, used for genres, forms and languages. The editorial intro is not optional — 150–250 hand-written words is the entire difference between a hub and a thin auto-generated list, and thin auto-generated lists are what get catalog sites demoted. Only the canonical hubs (/genre/x, /language/x, /language/x/<form>) are indexable; every other facet combination lives under /browse?… as noindex, follow, so crawlers walk the links without indexing 4,000 near-duplicates.

Measured blocker: GET /catalog/browse/genres returns [] in production today — not one seeded work has a genre attached, and /browse/forms returns only ["Novel"]. There is currently nothing to build a genre hub out of. Classifying the seed set against a ~40-genre closed vocabulary (plan §9.3) has to land before this page can exist.

Genre

Literary fiction

In Indian publishing the line between "literary" and "popular" was drawn late and unevenly — Thakazhi and Basheer were serialised in weekly magazines before they were canonised. What collects here is the fiction that was written to last: the village novels of Kuttanad and Kasaragod, the modernist break of Khasakkinte Ithihasam, and the generation of women writers — Sara Joseph, K. R. Meera — who spent the 2000s rewriting whose story a novel is allowed to be. 212 works, across 11 languages, the oldest from 1889.

മലയാളംMalayalam literature

Malayalam has one of the shortest and densest literary histories in India: a written novel tradition barely 140 years old, six Jnanpith laureates, and a readership that made a state of 35 million people the most book-buying in the country. It is also the Indian language whose fiction travels best — Chemmeen reached English in 1962, Goat Days reached it in 2012, and in between an entire canon was carried across by translators who are only now being credited on the cover. This is what we have catalogued: 418 works, 187 authors, 64 with translations, and the printings that carried them.

An editors' list

Twelve Malayalam novels to start with

If you've never read a Malayalam novel, the canon is intimidating and the recommendations are always the same three books. This is a route through it — ordered not by importance but by how easy they are to fall into, with a note on which translation to look for if you can't read the original.

Updated 4 August 2026·12 books·8 available in English
1
ബാല്യകാലസഖി

Balyakalasakhi

Vaikom Muhammad Basheer · 1944 · 96 pages · ★ 4.5

Ninety-six pages, and the first thirty will teach you more about how Malayalam prose sounds than any history of it. Basheer wrote the way people talk, which in 1944 was a scandal and is now the reason he's still read. In English: Childhood Friend, trans. R. E. Asher.

2
ചെമ്മീൻ

Chemmeen

Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai · 1956 · 218 pages · ★ 4.4

The one everyone names, and it earns it. A love story that is really a book about the sea and what a community will do to keep its bargain with it. In English: Narayana Menon's 1962 translation is stately; the Harper reissue is easier to find.

3
ആടുജീവിതം

Aadujeevitham

Benyamin · 2008 · 212 pages · ★ 4.6

The modern one. A Malayali migrant is enslaved on a Saudi goat farm and survives it; the novel became the biggest Malayalam bestseller of the century and then a film. Reads like a thriller, sits like a documentary. In English: Goat Days, trans. Joseph Koyippally — excellent.

4
ഖസാക്കിന്റെ ഇതിഹാസം

Khasakkinte Ithihasam

O. V. Vijayan · 1969 · 204 pages · ★ 4.7

The book that broke Malayalam fiction open, and the hardest one here — put it fourth, not first. Vijayan translated it himself thirty years later, which makes the English version a rare thing: a translation with the author's authority.

Reviews are the most IMDB-ish thing Kitabi has and they are currently invisible on the web — /catalog/works/{id}/reviews requires a signed-in user. W13

Mobile — where the traffic actually lands

Most search traffic to a book page arrives on a phone, from a Google result, on a mediocre connection. The mobile book page is not a squeezed desktop page: the cover shrinks, the rating moves up beside the title, and the translation module stays above the editions table because it is still the most differentiating thing on the page. The app band appears once, after the blurb — never as an interstitial, which Google penalises and readers hate.

Mobile decisionWhy
No interstitial, everGoogle demotes intrusive app interstitials on mobile search landings. The app appears as one inline band and one rail card — both dismissible by scrolling past.
Cover is the LCP elementfetchpriority="high", explicit width/height, no loading="lazy". Every other cover on the page is lazy.
Translation module stays highIt's the differentiator. Editions and reviews can wait; a reader who can't read Malayalam needs the English row before anything else.
Blurb clamps to 3 linesCSS line-clamp + a <details> fallback — the full text is in the HTML, so it's fully indexed even though it's visually collapsed.
Malayalam font loads only hereunicode-range on the @font-face means an English-only page never downloads the 42 KB Malayalam subset. The browser decides, not the template.
The app link is a door, not a wall. Every action a visitor might want — rate, review, shelve, lend — happens in the app. The web stays strictly read-only, which keeps RLS deny-by-default intact and the public attack surface at zero.
W14

The thin page, the empty search, and the 404

The counter-intuitive part of the plan, drawn. Do not index all 1,402 works. A large share of the seed has a transliteration-garbage title, no description, no cover and one edition — "Hīro-hīro warasip", arathāta, Kalādhārī, kalādhārīpūjā is a real row in production right now. Publishing a thousand pages like that teaches Google the domain produces thin pages, and drags down the two hundred good ones. That mechanism has killed more catalog sites than slow servers ever have.

So a work page is index, follow only if it clears a content floor — a cover, or a description ≥120 chars, or a review, or ≥2 editions, or a rating. Everything else is noindex, follow: still crawlable, still linked, still there for a human with the URL, just not competing. It flips to indexable automatically the moment a reader adds a cover or a blurb — which quietly turns the app's "Improve this entry" flow into the site's SEO engine.

The budget — measured today, enforced tomorrow

Not "make it fast" — numbers, with a CI job that fails the build when they're missed. The "today" column was measured against production on 4 August 2026.

MetricBudgetToday (measured)How it gets met
TTFB (cache hit)< 100 ms620 ms Render at the edge and cache.put() the HTML — s-maxage=300, stale-while-revalidate=86400. A crawler burst then hits Cloudflare, not Railway.
TTFB (cache miss)< 500 ms620 ms + 310 ms client fetch The one-payload rule: GET /public/book/<slug> returns the whole page in one call instead of four.
LCP (mobile 4G)< 1.2 s≈ 2.8 s SSR + self-hosted fonts + fetchpriority="high" on the cover, which is the LCP element.
CLS< 0.02unmeasured — no image dimensions Explicit width/height (or aspect-ratio: 2/3, which the design system already specifies) on every cover frame.
HTML, gzipped≤ 14 KB20.5 KB shell, no content 14 KB is the first congestion window — an above-fold document that fits arrives in one round trip.
Blocking requests01 CSS + 2 origin handshakes Drop fonts.googleapis.com. Self-host Fraunces (34 KB) + Inter (26 KB) subset woff2, preloaded; Noto Sans Malayalam (42 KB) behind unicode-range so English pages never fetch it. Saves an estimated 300–500 ms to first paint.
JS for content0 bytes100% of content The whole plan. ~4 KB deferred JS adds typeahead, the cover lightbox and histogram tooltips — each degrading to working plain HTML.
Third-party origins02 (Google Fonts, covers.openlibrary.org) Fonts self-hosted; covers proxied through /img/c/<id> with a 30-day immutable edge cache, backfilled into the existing Supabase covers bucket — no new store, no new credential.
A budget nobody measures is a wish. A Lighthouse CI step in .github/workflows/ runs against three sampled page types on every deploy and fails the build when LCP or document size regresses.
What none of this costs. Cloudflare Pages Functions, the existing FastAPI on Railway, the existing Supabase covers bucket. No framework, no build step, no new service, no new monthly bill, no new credential — CLAUDE.md rule 8 holds all the way through.

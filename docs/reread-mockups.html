Reading it again

Some books get read twice, or every year. Kitabi currently believes in one book, one read — and that belief is load-bearing in three places, so the feature is not a counter bolted on the side. It is the same move lending already made: a read becomes a record.

Area 1

What breaks today

Not a design question — a statement of the three things that make “just increment a number” impossible. 1. LibraryEntries has a singular startDate, finishDate and currentPage; a second pass has nowhere to put its own. 2. markBookFinished deliberately refuses to re-stamp a finish date — “the original date is the true one” — which is right for one read and wrong for many. 3. ReadingSessions hang off the entry, so a second pass’s sittings would merge into the first’s log with nothing to tell them apart, and autoFinishIfOnLastPage returns early on a book already marked read, so a re-read would never close itself. The fix for all three is one row.

Area 2

Starting again — and the status collision

Owner picked A · 29 Aug 2026

The hard question isn’t the button, it’s what a book is while you re-read it. It has genuinely been read, and it is genuinely being read. The shelf can only put it in one place, and the app asks that question hourly (“what am I in the middle of?”) while “have I read this?” is asked rarely and answered on the book page. Two directions, and a sheet that says out loud what carries over.

The cost of being in both
Shelf counts stop summing to 52. “3 Reading · 7 Read” now double-counts a book, and every filter has to decide whether this one is in or out.
Honest about the fact, and it never hides that you’ve read it. But it turns one clean question — where does this book stand? — into two answers, in the one place the app answers it fastest.

2c · Direction B — both, stacked (drawn, not taken) Two pills, and the book appears on both shelves. Never loses the fact that you’ve read it, even mid-re-read. Breaks the shelf arithmetic and forces a rule into every filter. I’d take A: the count is a fact about history, and history belongs on the book page, not the shelf.

Area 3

The reads themselves

Each pass owns its sittings and its notes. That is the whole reason to make a read a row: a note you wrote at twenty about a book you re-read at forty is not a note about this read — and merging the two logs loses the only interesting thing about re-reading, which is the difference.

4b · Home, and the one new card The live card carries the pass it belongs to. Under it, an optional nudge built only from ratings and dates. Deliberately not an AI surface — it is arithmetic on the reader’s own stars, so it sidesteps the recommendation opt-in entirely and can ship with the feature. Needs a frequency cap or it becomes a nag; one card, and never the same book twice in a season.

The data shape

One new syncable table, and two foreign keys. Reads — libraryEntryId, ordinal, startDate, finishDate, currentPage, status — plus the usual id / user_id / created_at / updated_at / deleted_at (rule 10). Then ReadingSessions.readId and ReadingNotes.readId, so a sitting and a note each name the pass they belong to.

This is rule 14 applied a second time. Lending is a record, not a flag, because “lent to X, on this date, returned ✓” is a fact with a shape. A read is the same: it has a beginning, an end, a pace and a set of thoughts, and squeezing it into three columns on the entry is exactly what makes the second one impossible.

What it costs elsewhere

  • markBookFinished stops being idempotent-by-refusal. Today it declines to re-stamp finishDate because “the original date is the true one”. That comment stays correct — it just belongs to the read now, not the entry. Finishing stamps the current read.
  • autoFinishIfOnLastPage returns early when status == 'read', so a re-read would never close itself on the last page. It must test the current read, not the book.
  • The entry keeps its columns as a mirror of the current read rather than losing them. Every existing reader of currentPage — the four progress surfaces, the timer, the Live Activity, the web pages — keeps working untouched, which is the difference between a migration and a rewrite.
  • Backfill is one row per finished entry. Ordinal 1, dates copied across, existing sittings and notes adopted into it. A book with a finish date but no sittings becomes the honest “no timings kept” row in 1b — which is why that state is drawn rather than hidden.
  • Ratings stay on the Work (rule 13, rule 17) — one number, not one per read. The “★★★★★ after this read” line in 3a needs only a timestamp on the rating, matched against the read that was open at the time. Cheap, and skippable in v1.
  • Sync: nothing new. A read is an ordinary syncable row under the existing engine, and the conflict rules already cover it. The one thing to get right is that ordinal must be derived on read, never stored as a bare counter, or two devices starting a re-read offline both write “third”.

What I'd build

  • Direction A for the status collision — one pill, gold eyebrow. Chosen, 29 Aug 2026. B is more honest and costs the shelf arithmetic, which the app relies on far more often than it states the read count. The consequence to accept with A: while a re-read is open the book is only on the Reading shelf, so a reader filtering Read mid-re-read will not find it. The count itself never vanishes — it is on the cover tally and the book page the whole time.
  • The tally (1c), the reads list (3a) and per-read logs and notes (3b) are the feature. Everything in Area 4 is a consequence, and can follow.
  • The pace comparison in 3c and the “worth another look” card in 4b are the delight, and both are arithmetic on rows that already exist — no model, no new bill (rule 8).

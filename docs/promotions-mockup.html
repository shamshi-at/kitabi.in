In-app promotions

A banner and a card that appear only when you publish one from the console — targeted by the languages a reader actually reads, written once per language, dismissible always, and gone the moment the campaign ends. First-party only: no ad SDK, no advertising identifier, no new bill. Design doc: docs/promotions-plan.md.

1 · The default

With nothing published, Home is exactly Home

The promotion system's most common state is off. No slot, no placeholder, no reserved height, no "no promotions right now" — the widgets simply aren't in the tree. This is the same rule that just bit "Fresh on your shelf": a heading over an empty space reads as a broken app, not an empty one.

2 · Surface one

The banner — a strip, not a box

One line, one optional mark, one dismiss. It truncates instead of wrapping, so a long headline can never grow the strip and shove the currently-reading card off screen. Three tones, and the tone is chosen by what the promo is, not by whoever writes it: gold for Kitabi's own news, slate for a sponsored placement, oxblood for the rare operational notice that can't be dismissed.

The three tones, and the overflow caseThe 3px coloured rule is a child Container inside a uniformly-bordered, clipped box — never a non-uniform Border() with a borderRadius, which throws at paint time and renders a blank rectangle (CLAUDE.md, 21 Jul 2026). Only the operational tone may set dismissible: false.
What the banner is for

One sentence a reader can act on or ignore in under a second: an event, a new platform, a sale, a notice. It is not for anything that needs explaining — that's the card. If a banner needs a second line, it was the wrong surface.

Rules the code enforces
One at a time Highest priority wins; ties break on the older starts_at. A deterministic rule beats a random one when you're debugging "why didn't mine show".
Impression on sight Counted when the widget is actually visible, never on fetch — otherwise every metric in §11 is a lie.
Cooldown min_hours_between, default 24. The same strip on every cold start is how a promo becomes a grievance.
Dies locally expires_at is in the cached row, so an ended campaign vanishes offline too.
Never over reading No promo surface exists on the reading timer, the book page's Yours tab, or any modal. Interrupting a sitting is not a growth tactic.
3 · Surface two

The card — three shapes, all drawn by Kitabi

The card sits in the Home stream where the recommendations entry already sits, with the same weight. An advertiser never controls the frame — they supply an image, a headline, two lines and a link, and Kitabi draws the rest. That's the difference between a promotion and an ad slot, and it's the whole reason the app can carry one without looking cheap.

4 · The headline feature

A Malayalam promo reaches Malayalam readers — in Malayalam

Two mechanisms that people usually conflate, kept separate on purpose. Targeting decides who is eligible. Content variants decide which words they see. One campaign carries both language versions, and each reader gets theirs — so you write the promo once, not twice, and you don't end up with two campaigns whose schedules drift apart. The source is profiles.preferred_languages: what the reader chose at onboarding, the same field that already filters Discover.

5 · Disclosure

Labelled, always, in the app's own small-caps voice

Every promotion carries a label in the same treatment as CURRENTLY READING and YOUR SHELVES — it belongs to the page rather than shouting over it. No sponsor set → "From Kitabi". Sponsor set → "Sponsored · {name}". There is no third option and no way to publish without one; the label is derived from the data, not typed by the author.

Two labels, and one switchNobody requires the opt-out. But an app whose pitch is that it isn't the attention economy, which then makes its own promos unavoidable, is arguing with itself. Turning it off filters at the API, so the device stops receiving campaigns rather than silently downloading and hiding them.
Why this matters more than it looks
Store policy Both stores require paid placements to be identifiable as advertising. A derived label can't be forgotten by whoever writes the campaign at 11pm.
No ATT prompt Targeting reads the reader's own languages and library — no advertising identifier, no cross-app tracking, so no App Tracking Transparency dialog and no tracking domains in the iOS privacy manifest. Play's Data Safety form barely changes.
Never sensitive Language here is a reading preference the reader typed in. Keep it that way: no targeting rule may be built that turns it into a proxy for anything else.
Say it in the policy One paragraph on the existing privacy page — that Kitabi shows its own promotions, chosen server-side from language and library, and counts engagement. Ships in the same release as the feature, not after it.
6 · Interaction

Dismissal is instant, offline, and permanent

Tapping ✕ writes dismissedAt to Drift and the widget is gone on the next frame — no network in the path, because the one thing worse than an unwanted promo is an unwanted promo that won't close on a bad connection. The event goes to a small local outbox and drains later, so the server stops serving it to that reader's other devices too.

The four statesCollapse is a 150ms size transition, not a fade — the surrounding content moving up is what tells the reader it worked.
What happens underneath
Drift, now dismissedAt = now() on the cached row. The StreamProvider re-emits, Home rebuilds without it. No await, no spinner.
Outbox A dismiss event with a client-generated uuid into PromotionEventQueue — so a retried batch is idempotent, exactly as the sync engine's op UUIDs already work.
Not the sync queue Promo events are append-only facts with no updates, no deletes, no conflicts. Putting them through sync_queue would drag server_seq cursors, LWW and conflict-history rows into a case that needs none of them — and would put marketing telemetry in the same retry path as a reader's library, where a promo hiccup could stall real data.
Drop, don't hoard Five attempts, exponential backoff, then discard. A lost impression count is not worth a queue that never empties.
Across devices Once the event lands, the serve query excludes it everywhere. Until then the reader's other phone may show it once more — the honest cost of offline-first, written down here so nobody files it as a bug later.
Frequency, if you don't want to rely on dismissal
MAX IMPRESSIONS
Stop after N views by that reader. Default off.
COOLDOWN
Hours between showings. Default 24 — not zero, deliberately.
RE-SHOW AFTER
Days before a dismissed promo may return. Default never.

Notes

What these mockups commit us to

The shape of it
Three tables, one migration promotions (the campaign), promotion_contents (copy, one row per language, null = default), promotion_events (append-only impression/click/dismiss). Next in sequence: 000034_promotions. Server-only, not syncable, RLS on with zero policies.
Targeting resolves on the server Always. The device receives only what it's eligible for — a campaign it isn't in never reaches it. That's the privacy argument, and it also means you can retarget without shipping an app release.
Two mechanisms, not one Targeting picks the audience; content variants pick the words. Conflating them gives you N campaigns per language with drifting schedules.
Render from Drift Cached like the catalog, so promos work offline and expire on the device. The provider is a StreamProvider over a Drift watch — a FutureProvider here would mean dismissal doesn't update Home, which is the same bug this repo has already shipped three times.
Its own event outbox Not sync_queue. Append-only facts need no cursors, no LWW, no conflict history — and marketing telemetry must never share a retry path with a reader's library.
One banner, one card, never more Capped in the serve query, before the payload exists, so no client bug can stack them.
Still to decide
Sponsored placements? The field ships either way. The answer changes the label and whether store "paid content" rules engage. Safe default: build it, run only Kitabi's own promos at launch.
Images — R2 or paste a URL? R2 recommended: the Cloudflare account and credentials already exist for nightly backups, so a second bucket is no new service and no new bill. Pasting a URL is zero work and fine while you're the only author. The same bucket would later serve reader cover photos — the upload gap that's been an open decision since Phase 2.
The opt-out toggle Nobody requires it. Recommendation: ship it — see §5.
May a promo ever interrupt? This design says no: no modals, no takeovers, nothing over the reading timer or the book page. If a launch-day announcement needs more reach than a Home banner, that's what a non-dismissible banner is for.
And the part that isn't a mockup
The last three owner-reported bugs in this repo — the timer's page save, the Live Activity deep link, the wax-seal styling — all passed their tests and failed on the phone. A promotions system's entire job is to render correctly on somebody else's device. The definition of done is not a green test run: it's a real campaign, created in the console, appearing on a real phone, dismissed, and gone.

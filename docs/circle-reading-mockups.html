Your circle is reading

The most persuasive line a book can carry isn’t a star average from strangers — it’s “Anu loved this.” Kitabi already knows who your circle is: connections are mutual and consented, mostly people you lend books to. This shows what they read, rated and own — only for connections who chose to share it, never one book they kept to themselves — in four places: search, the book page, a sheet behind it, and one quiet card on Home.

Area 1

Where a book is being chosen

Search and the book page are the two moments a reader is deciding. The circle earns one line in a search row and one card on the book page — always a sentence, always with faces, and absent entirely when there’s nothing to say. No “0 connections”, no empty card, no greyed placeholder. And on a book that also has a copy on a connection’s shelf, the card turns into Kitabi’s own wedge: ask to borrow instead of buy.

1b · Book page — not on your shelf The circle card sits right under Add to library: it’s the reason to tap it. A headline written as a sentence, the faces, the circle’s own average (labelled as theirs, kept apart from the catalogue’s 4.3), one public review line, and — when a connection owns it — the lending door. Gold strip = the same accent as lending, because “ask Faisal” is lending. “Ask to borrow” opens the existing lending request; nothing new to learn.

1d · “See all” sheet Grouped by what each person did, not a feed. Each name is a door to their profile, like every name in Kitabi. A connection who shares their reading but not their ratings shows as “no rating shared” — the sheet never guesses. “Read it twice” comes free from rule 19 — a read is a record. Translations are their own Works, so the footer bridges them: the regional angle, made social.

Area 2

One card on Home, never a feed

Home gets one “From your circle” card — the same discipline as the AI pick (“one labelled pick on Home, never a feed”). It draws from what connections finished and rated 4★ or more in the last 60 days, skips books already on your shelf, and stays the same all day (seeded by date) so it doesn’t shuffle every time you open the app. It’s styled as a letter, not an ad: a wax-seal initial, the friend’s own words if they made a review public, and two doors — Want to read and, when they own it, Ask to borrow.

2c · Before you’ve opted in The honest invitation: it shows a count of people who already chose to share (their consent, so the count is theirs to reveal) and asks you to do the same. It’s the moment the feature proves its worth, which is why the opt-in lives here and not in a launch pop-up. No empty states anywhere else — silence is the empty state. Built amendment (13 Oct 2026): when nobody shares yet it still invites a reader who has connections — “Share your reading with your 3 connections” — or nobody would ever go first.

Area 3

Privacy — what each connection agreed to

Today Kitabi has no “connections-only” tier: library_visible means public, and an individual’s star rating is never shown to anyone — it only feeds the book’s average. So this feature can’t borrow an existing switch; it needs its own consent, off by default, asked at the moment of value. Three separate things to share, because they’re three different disclosures — and a per-book “keep this one to myself”, because the book a reader least wants their circle to see is exactly the one a blanket switch can’t protect.

3c · Per book: keep this one to myself On the Yours tab (and optionally at add time, so a book never flashes into anyone’s view first). It’s a property of your copy — the library entry — so it syncs like everything else on it and works offline. The one honest exception is spelled out: a public review is public.

The privacy rules, in order of precedence

  1. Only accepted connections. Pending, denied and blocked rows never qualify; blocking someone removes both directions at once. Strangers and public visitors see nothing new — the public web is untouched.
  2. Only what the person ticked. Three separate switches on the sharer’s profile: reading (status + finished), ratings, ownership. All default off.
  3. Never a book they kept to themselves. A per-entry flag beats every switch.
  4. Reviews keep their own flag. Only reviews already marked public are quoted; this feature can’t make a private review visible.
  5. Never shared, regardless of switches: notes, wishlist / to-read, pages and progress, reading time, lending history, tags. (Read-along ② uses your To-read and their “reading” — your wishlist is never shown to them.)
  6. Counts only count consenters. “4 connections read this” is four people who agreed to be counted; there is no “and 2 who hid it”. A count can therefore never reveal a hidden read.
  7. Enforced on the server, in one query. The app never receives a row it shouldn’t display, so there is nothing to filter client-side and nothing to leak from a cache.
  8. No pushes. “Anu finished a book” is not worth a notification. The circle is ambient, never interruptive — in keeping with the Reading Room voice.

Endpoints — and why they stay cheap

  • POST /circle/signals {work_ids:[…≤50]} → per work: {read, reading, owns, names[≤2], avg}. One round trip for a whole search page (1a). Payload is a few hundred bytes — the Sep 2026 egress lesson applies.
  • GET /circle/works/{work_id} → the full card + sheet (1b, 1c, 1d), including the translation bridge.
  • GET /circle/pick → zero or one Home card (2a/2b), chosen server-side, seeded by user_id + date so it’s stable all day; dismissed ids are sent along so the server skips them.
  • Every query starts from the caller’s accepted connections who opted in — a handful of user ids — then hits library_entries / ratings by user_id = ANY(:ids), already indexed. No fan-out, no precomputed feed table, no job. Wrap in ttl_cache per caller (≈10 min) so search typing doesn’t re-query.
  • Offline: this is cross-user data, so it follows rule 2 (server-authoritative, cached for reading) rather than the sync engine. Cache the last answer per work in Drift with its fetch time; show it for up to a day offline, then show nothing. The UI never blocks on it — the card simply appears when the answer lands.
  • Reciprocity check (if adopted) is the first line of each endpoint: a caller with all three switches off gets {"invite": n} instead of signals, which is exactly what 2c renders.

Decisions for the owner

Taken 13 Oct 2026: the owner went with every recommendation below, and the feature is built (docs/tasks.md Phase C). Built as drawn except: the “Kept to myself · N” list, the “See what Anu sees” preview and the add-sheet checkbox (3b/3c) are still open, and circle answers are cached in memory for two minutes rather than a day in Drift.

1 · Reciprocity — see your circle only if you share? Recommended yes (any one switch on is enough). It’s fair, it explains itself in one sentence, and it’s the honest nudge out of the off-by-default trap that would otherwise leave the feature dark for almost everyone (the recaps_visible note in profile.py names the same risk).
2 · Defaults — off for everyone, asked once? Recommended off, all three, asked via 2c. Connections are consented, so “on by default for connections” is defensible — but it would retroactively expose ratings that every reader gave believing they were anonymous. That’s the line: a rating given privately must not become attributed without a fresh yes.
3 · Should circle signals re-rank search? Recommended no. Annotate, don’t reorder — search answers the query. (Browse/discovery could later offer a “Loved by your circle” shelf, which is a different surface with a different promise.)
4 · Ratings on a book you’re mid-way through? Recommended: show faces and “read this”, but hold back their stars and review quotes while your status is Reading, with a one-tap “show anyway”. A five-star from a friend mid-book is pressure; a two-star is a spoiler of mood.
5 · Scope tag. This is the first slice of “Follow users / public profiles” [LATER] in feature-map.md — but limited to the existing, consented connection graph. Suggest adding it as its own line (e.g. “Circle signals — connection-only, opt-in”) tagged for the phase you want, plus tasks in docs/tasks.md, before building.

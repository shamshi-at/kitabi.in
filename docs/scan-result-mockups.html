The moment after the beep

The scanner reads a barcode, finds a book — and then almost nothing happens. A quiet strip slides in under a still-bright camera, naming a book you can’t open, behind a gold button that says Add and nothing else. Three directions for the result, four ways to make it land, a rack of labels for the button, and the four states that aren’t “found it”.

Direction A

The Slip — the lights go down, one page stays lit

The camera dims to almost nothing and the found book rises over it as a paper slip — the only lit surface on a dark screen, which is the whole highlight, done with light rather than a border. The slip is a door: cover, title, author and printing are one big tap target with a chevron, and a dashed line under them spells out both ways out. The camera is still there, dimmed, so “that’s not my book” is a tap on the dark half.

The landing

Four ways to say “got it” — pick one, it’s orthogonal to the direction

“It should highlight” is its own decision. Each of these works with any of A, B or C, and each pairs with one selection haptic. Under prefers-reduced-motion every one of them still has a static end state — the gold frame, the dim, the stamp, the filled cover — so nothing depends on the animation being seen.

H1 · The gold snap
9788129142146

The reticle contracts onto the barcode and pulses gold once, and the ISBN turns from grey text into a gold pill. Cheapest to build, reads as “the machine locked on” — the language every scanner in the world already uses.

The label

“Add” — to what?

Shown at roughly device scale. The word has to survive being read by someone who has never used the app, standing in a bookshop, holding a book. Two things it must not be confused with: adding a book to the shared catalogue (a different, public act, one screen away) and adding it to a wishlist.

Today
Add

Names no object. In a flow whose title is literally “Add a book” it could plausibly mean “add it to the catalogue”, which is the one thing it doesn’t do. It’s also the only gold-filled button in the app that isn’t a catalogue contribution.

Option 1 · the app’s existing words
❦ Add to my library

Already the label on every unowned book page (bookAddToLibrary), already translated, already the leather-and-foil plate readers meet there. Consistency beats novelty: the scanner should not invent a second name for the app’s most common action.

Option 2 · the physical claim
Put it on my shelf

The most concrete, and closest to the product’s wedge — Kitabi tracks ownership, not just reading. Costs a new string and a small divergence from the book page, unless that page changes too.

Option 3 · the possessive
❦ This copy is mine

Says the quiet part: a scan is proof you’re holding it. Reads beautifully next to Wishlist (want vs. have), and it makes the printing question feel natural — this copy. Weakest when a reader is scanning a friend’s shelf or a bookshop table.

The pair, in place
❦Add to my library
Wishlist

Whatever the primary says, the second action is what removes the ambiguity: with Wishlist beside it, the primary can only mean “I have this”. That contrast does more work than any single wording.

Beneath both, one line of consequence — “Rupa 2016, 280 pp — the printing you scanned” — so the button never has to carry the whole meaning alone.

The other four

Not-found, offline, already-yours, no-camera

Drawn in Direction A’s slip, because whichever direction wins, these four have to be the same object as the success state — a scanner that has one beautiful path and four grey ones is the screen we already have. Each says what happened, why, and what the reader can do next; none is a dead end.

What changes in code

  • Theme(data: buildNightOverlayTheme()) stops wrapping the whole screen and wraps only the camera state; the result states render under the app’s own theme.
  • The private _night* constants disappear from the result widgets, which switch to AppColors — so they follow light/dark for free, and the palette lives in one place.
  • StatusPill, the genre chips and the progress line are used as-is instead of re-tinted copies; PaperPlate drops its onNight flag on this screen.
  • The tests barely move: they assert text and callbacks, not colour.

The one real question

Going from a dark camera to a paper result is a bright flash in a dim room — the exact moment a reader is standing in a bookshop at night. Worth trying on the device before committing; if it stings, the fix is a short cross-fade rather than keeping the result dark, because the reader’s next screen (the book page) is paper either way.

What I’d build

Direction A (The Slip) + landing H2, with the printing line from B. It fixes all three complaints without a new screen or new state: the result lands hard because the camera goes dark behind a lit page; the whole card is a door onto the book page that already exists and already handles a book you don’t own; and the button becomes the same leather-and-foil Add to my library the reader will meet on every unowned book, with Wishlist beside it doing the work of disambiguating it.

Direction B is the better screen and the worse flow — I’d keep its printing block and its “3 others ›” affordance, dropped into A’s slip, and leave the rest of B on the book page where it already lives. Direction C is a different feature (bulk cataloguing) worth its own decision later; C′ is the cheap half of it and could ride along as a “keep scanning” preference.

What it touches

  • app/lib/features/catalog/presentation/isbn_scan_screen.dart — _ConfirmCard becomes the slip: a tappable body pushing Routes.bookDetailPath(workId, editionId), the dim over MobileScanner, and the found/miss/offline branches sharing one container.
  • The two plates already exist as _LeatherPlate / _PaperPlate in book_detail_screen.dart — they should move to core/widgets/ and be used by both, or the scanner and the book page will drift the way the four progress surfaces did.
  • New strings only for the eyebrows, the consequence line and the offline copy; bookAddToLibrary, bookWishlistShort and editionPickTitle are reused as they are.
  • chooseEdition stays the fallback for “3 others ›”; the scanned printing is already resolved server-side via scanned_edition_id, so the slip can name it without a lookup.
  • The form-mode scanner (returnResult == true) keeps the same slip with one action swapped to Use these details — one widget, two callers, so they can’t drift.

Open questions for you

  • Primary label: Add to my library (consistent) or Put it on my shelf / This copy is mine (better voice, and then the book page should change too)?
  • Should the scanner offer Wishlist at all? It’s the thing that makes the primary unambiguous, but you’re usually holding the book when you scan it.
  • Is bulk scanning (C) a real need for you now, or is C′’s “keep scanning” enough?

Kitabi Supporter

A membership, not a paywall. Supporters get the Kitabi seal — a small gold stamp beside their name — and a bigger share of the AI budget. Everyone else gets the app they already have, and is asked for help at most three times a year, never before day 14, never during a reading session, and never twice if they said no. 1 · The default

For a reader who never pays, nothing changes

This is the state the feature lives in 99% of the time, and the one it has to get right first. There is no locked feature, no greyed-out seal where a seal would go, no "upgrade" chip in the header, no red dot on the tab bar, no empty slot. Absence is never marked. The only permanent trace of the whole programme in a free reader's app is one row near the bottom of Profile — below the visibility switches, above sign-out — which never nags and is never counted as an ask.

2 · The mark

The seal — a wax stamp, not a tick

A small gold disc carrying the app's own fleuron ❦. It reads at 13px, it belongs to the Reading Room, and — the part that matters — it does not collide with any mark already in use. Kitabi's visual language is already carrying four meanings in gold; a fifth had to be a shape none of them use. A checkmark was the obvious choice and the wrong one: a tick means verified identity everywhere on earth, and this mark means paid.

3 · Disclosure

The seal is tappable, and says what it isn't

A gold mark beside a stranger's name invites exactly one wrong guess: Kitabi checked this person. One sentence prevents it, so the seal is a tap target everywhere it appears — profile, review, search row — and what it opens leads with the meaning and states the limit plainly. This is cheap insurance on the one risk in the whole feature.

3b · The lend sheet, seal-free by design. The single hardest rule in the plan, drawn rather than described. Trust marks and trust transactions stay apart.

Why this document keeps saying "not verified". The brief called it a verified badge, and the word is worth ₹149 precisely because people read it as vetted. But Kitabi's wedge is that readers hand each other physical books — and authors.linked_user_id plus the author_claims queue already exist to mean this really is them. One word, two meanings, one of them purchasable, is a collision that gets more expensive every month it stands.

The recommendation is a two-word change, not a redesign: the mark is gold, it's on the profile, other readers see it, and people will want it — it's called Supporter, and "Verified" stays free, earned, and reserved for identity. If the answer is still "Verified", then 3a and 3b stop being belt-and-braces and become the two screens that hold the whole thing up.

10 · Public web

The web page explains it; the app sells it

The membership is an in-app subscription on both stores (owner decision, 9 Aug 2026) — so it's bound to the Kitabi account, it renews itself, and it needs no admin to switch anything on. That puts a hard constraint on this page: it may describe the membership, but it must not sell it or link to a way of buying it. Anti-steering cuts both ways — an external purchase link inside the app is a 3.1.1 rejection, and a purchase path out here that undercut the stores' would be the same argument in reverse. So the page ends where every Kitabi page ends: get the app. It still earns its place — this is what a search result, a shared link, or somebody asking "what is that gold thing next to your name?" lands on.

10b · The seal on the public web. Rendered server-side by the same Pages Function that already draws this page — one extra field on the existing /public/ payload, no client JS, so a crawler sees it too. The footer line from 10a ("not an identity check") is the web's version of the tap sheet in 3a: the disclosure follows the mark wherever it goes.

Why the money moves through the stores, at a 15% cost. ₹149 by UPI would arrive whole — UPI has no MDR — but it can only be linked to a reader by hand: an anonymous page, a payment with no callback, and somebody remembering to mail their handle. In-app subscriptions bind the purchase to the Kitabi account three ways (supporter-plan §10.4), renew themselves, and let a reader cancel without asking anyone. ~₹127 instead of ₹149 is the price of it being real, and at 200 supporters that difference is ₹4,400 a year — less than the cost of one wrong manual reconciliation.

12b · Grant complimentary — the brief's "badge without a subscription". Four durations, a required reason, and a default of silent. "Extended, never overwritten" is the rule that stops a well-meant grant from shortening somebody's paid year — the arithmetic is in the dialog, not in the admin's head. No expiry exists for one honest case — library pilots and the invited writer circle — and it's the option that shows up in an audit, which is why it's last and not default.

13 · Console

The two paths that don't go through a store

Store subscriptions need no console at all — they verify, write their own ledger row, and renew themselves. These two screens are for everything else. Record a payment is the escape hatch for the case that will keep happening anyway: somebody hands you ₹149 directly, or a store purchase fails to reconcile and a reader is out of pocket. Revoke is the other end. Neither is a growth feature; both are what stops a support mail from becoming a database session.

13a · Record a payment — the exception, not the path. Reader lookup, amount, reference, period. Because this one can't renew itself, the line that matters is the last: the expiry reminder is queued at the moment the money is recorded, not left to somebody's memory a year later.

Why there's still no payment gateway. A Razorpay/Cashfree integration for a ₹149 product is a new credential, a new monthly relationship, webhook handling, refund flows and PCI-adjacent questions — rule 8 says no. With the stores carrying every normal purchase, a gateway would exist purely to automate the handful of exceptions this screen already covers in twenty seconds.

Revoke Supporter

Why is this being taken back?
What this does: the seal disappears, and the daily AI caps drop back to 20 / 40.
What it doesn't: nothing else. No book, note, review, loan, shelf or export is affected — revoking a membership is not a suspension.
The reader is not notified. If money was taken, refund it yourself first; this button doesn't move money.
The grant row is kept and marked revoked — the history stays readable, and the action is reversible by granting again.
Super admin only
CancelRevoke
Two different buttons, deliberately far apart. Revoke ends a membership. Hide seal (on the reader card) removes the mark while the membership runs — the moderation case where someone is using the seal badly, which is not the same as taking their year away. And neither is Suspend, which already exists and locks the account. Three different severities, three different buttons, each saying exactly what it does.
Notes

The data model, and what these mockups commit us to

Small: one column does the entitlement, one table keeps the receipts. Nothing here is syncable — profiles is the identity row, online-only — so the app caches supporter_until in Drift purely so the seal and the caps survive a flight.

profiles — the entitlement + the ask budget, enforced server-side + supporter_until timestamptz null null = not a supporter + supporter_since timestamptz null drives “Supporter since 2026” + Founding no. + supporter_badge_hidden boolean not null default false ← the reader's own switch + supporter_asked_at timestamptz null last ask shown — the 60-day gap + supporter_asks_count int not null default 0 the 3-a-year cap + supporter_never_ask boolean not null default false ← survives a reinstall supporter_grants — append-only ledger. never edited, never deleted id uuid pk user_id uuid → profiles source text 'ios' | 'android' | 'upi' | 'admin' amount_inr int null null for a complimentary grant period_start timestamptz period_end timestamptz null null = no expiry (a lifetime grant) reason text null REQUIRED when source = 'admin' granted_by uuid null → admins revoked_at timestamptz null revoked_by uuid null → admins created_at timestamptz — store-backed rows only: store_txn_id text null ← UNIQUE with source: a replayed receipt is a no-op store_original_id text null ← UNIQUE across users: one sub can't light up two accounts auto_renewing boolean not null default false raw_status text null last status seen from the store, for support supporter_until = max(period_end) over live grants — recomputed on every grant, renewal and revoke. Never hand-edited. RLS deny-by-default, zero policies, like every table. llm_quota.quota_for() takes one new argument: is_supporter.
What these mockups commit us to
  • The seal is a gold ❦ stamp, it means supports Kitabi, and it is tappable everywhere it appears — with a sheet that names what it is and what it isn't.
  • "Verified" stays reserved for identity (author and publisher claims), is earned rather than bought, and the two marks are never merged.
  • No seal on any lending or borrowing surface, ever. Trust marks stay out of trust transactions.
  • Absence is never marked. A free reader's screens are identical to today's, apart from one row in Profile.
  • The ask is a promotion — Phase 9's pipeline, Phase 9's dismissal store, Phase 9's opt-out — with the harder caps in Area 8 enforced server-side.
  • "Don't ask again" is permanent, lives on the profile, silences every surface, and survives a reinstall.
  • Every ask carries a second door that costs no money, and that door feeds the catalogue.
  • Every free feature stays free — lending, library, sync, import and export above all. Supporter raises caps; it never lowers a floor.
  • Grants are a ledger: append-only, reasoned, audited, reversible. Complimentary seals look identical to bought ones to everyone except this console.
  • The membership is bought in the app on both stores and bound to the Kitabi account three ways — stamped store-side (appAccountToken / obfuscatedAccountId), verified through an authenticated call, and made unfarmable by a unique store_original_id. The client's purchase token is a claim; only the server writes an entitlement.
  • No Cloud Pub/Sub. Apple's App Store Server Notifications are a plain HTTPS webhook; Google's equivalent needs a Pub/Sub topic, so Google gets polled by the APScheduler job that already exists. Symmetry is not worth a new service (rule 8).
  • The public web explains the membership and never sells it — anti-steering cuts both ways, and every page still ends at "get the app".

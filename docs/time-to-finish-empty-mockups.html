“≈ 149 weeks at your rate”

On a 176-page screenplay, from one sitting of one minute. The number is arithmetic working perfectly on a sample that means nothing — and it is labelled “at your rate”, which is the part that costs trust: the reader has no rate. Four ways to say so instead.

The problem

Three figures, one of them absurd

The block already knows it is guessing — it says NOT YOURS YET and at a typical 40 pages/hour. Then it prints two derived figures anyway, and the weeks tile silently switches authority to “at your rate”. Hours from a stated typical rate is a defensible guess. Weeks needs a weekly volume, which one minute of history cannot supply.

The rule I’d apply, whichever direction wins

The bug isn’t the arithmetic, it’s that every figure is printed at the same confidence. Each one needs a different amount of evidence, so each should have its own gate:

  • Hours — safe with no data at all, provided it names whose pace it used. It divides pages by a pace; a stated typical rate is a real answer to “how long is this book”.
  • Sittings — needs a believable sitting length. One minute is not one. Require ≥3 sittings and drop any sitting under ~5 minutes from the median.
  • Weeks — needs weekly volume, the noisiest input of the three: it depends on how often you read, not how fast. Require at least 2 calendar weeks of history. This is the tile that printed 149 and it should be the last to unlock, not the one wearing the gold border.

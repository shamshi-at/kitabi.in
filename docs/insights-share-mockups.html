The answer, ready to send

"How's your reading going?" is a question that arrives in person and on WhatsApp — never inside the app. Today the answer sits behind four taps and a 16-pixel glyph, and the image that finally goes out drops the chart — the streak, the bars, the heatmap stay home while a bare numeral travels. This redesign makes Insights a stack of finished, share-shaped cards: what you see is the pixel-for-pixel image you send, every period's own visualization rides on it, and sending is one labelled tap. Three directions for the page, one redesigned card family, and a new chat-sized format for the place the question is actually asked.

The brief

What today's design gets wrong — and right

The 27 Jul flagship-card redesign (10a–10j) got the data right: one card per window, a sentence before a number, quiet literary register. What it under-built is the door out. Four faults, each visible in the shipped app: 1 · The share affordance is furniture. A 16px ⇲ glyph in the card corner — tertiary chrome for what the owner now names as the page's whole job. 2 · The chart never travels. PeriodShareCard is typography-only: the streak dots, the week's bars, the month's heatmap — the part of the answer that makes someone say "oh nice" — are all stripped at the moment of sharing. 3 · Preview ≠ page. The sheet re-lays-out the data into a different card, so the thing the reader admired on screen is not the thing that goes out. 4 · The year shares as a numeral. The most shareable window a reader has is a shelf of actual books, and it leaves the app as "24". Five rules govern everything below: the page is the answer (every card is drawn share-shaped; the wordmark appears only on capture); the chart travels; send is a labelled button, never a glyph; chat first, story second — the question arrives in a message thread, so a compact slip joins Story/Square; and nothing private leaks — numbers, covers, titles only, never notes or private reviews, and the sheet says so.

Direction A

The postcard — the card is the artifact

The period chips stay (they earned their keep), but the flagship card becomes a poster: the double gold frame, corner fleurons and vignette of the share-card family, drawn at full width on the page itself. Under it, one gold-foil Send this card — the same foil object the scan result's Bookplate uses for its primary, so "the important door" already has a house look. Below, a rack of smaller cards — the streak alone, the finished books as a shelf, the superlative — because the best answer to "how's it going" is sometimes a detail, not the summary. Everything in the rack is itself a card: same frame, same one-tap send.

A3 · Year — the shelf The redesign's centrepiece. Twenty-four books drawn as twenty-four spines, width proportional to pages, standing on the gold ledge the Shelves wall already uses — a real bookshelf's worth of year, not a numeral. Spines are anonymous colour by default (tints hashed from each title, the generated-cover trick); the sheet's toggle can name them. The goal ring isn't gone — it moves to the rack, because "80% of a target" is a private pace fact and "24 spines" is the public brag. The detail charts (bars, donut, superlatives) keep living below the fold, unchanged. Pro: an ownable, screenshot-proof visual — nobody else's year looks like yours.

Direction B

The almanac — the page itself is the print

No boxes at all. The screen becomes a printed almanac page: a heavy top rule, small-caps section heads, ledger rows with dotted leaders, Fraunces numerals set right — the whole page reads as one typeset sheet, and the whole page is what you share (captured as a single tall image, the format stories are made for). The one control is a gold wax seal — the house's fifth gold mark, borrowed with care — pressed bottom-right: ⇲ SEND. Long-press any single row to lift just that line out as a slip.

Direction C

The answer — lead with the question

The most literal reading of the goal: the page opens with the question itself, set in Fraunces italic like the literary quotes the app already rotates — and everything under it is the reply. The primary action is Reply with your reading, which opens the sheet already in slip format with the caption ready, because the question this page answers arrives in a chat thread. The period chips become "which answer": today's, this week's, the year's.

The slip

A card sized for the place the question is asked

Story and Square are broadcast shapes. But "how's your reading going?" arrives as a message, and a 9:16 story pasted into a chat thread is a poster taped over a conversation. The slip is the third format: landscape, bubble-sized, one number, one visual, the wordmark small — designed to sit inside a reply next to a line of your own words. It's the default format when the sheet opens from "Reply with your reading", and the caption travels as the message text, not baked into pixels.

The sheet

The share sheet — what you saw is what you send

The sheet stops re-laying-out the data into a different card. It shows the same card the page showed, adds the wordmark, and offers exactly three decisions: format (Story · Square · Slip, as shape-true chips), whether to name names ("Name the spines" / "Show covers" where the card has books), and the caption. One privacy sentence, always present, states the contract. Copy caption and Send stay the two-button footer the whole share family already uses — captureAndShareCard at 3× is untouched underneath.

States

The days there's nothing to brag about

Choosing

What to pick, and what ships regardless

Four pieces are direction-independent and worth building whichever page wins: the labelled foil Send replacing the ⇲ glyph; visualizations on the shared image (one card widget with Story/Square/Slip layouts, so page and sheet render the same object and fault №3 dies by construction); the slip format plus its "caption as message text" behaviour; and the year shelf, which is new derived data only in the narrowest sense (finished books × page counts — both already in PeriodSummary.booksFinished via the edition). The honest-state rules (no shareable zeroes, "still worth sending") also travel.

The page direction is the real decision. A recommendation, held loosely: A with C's top line grafted on — the postcard page, opened by the question set small above the chips — keeps today's information architecture (chips, flagship, detail below the fold on Year), which means the redesign lands as one screen rebuilt rather than a navigation change; B stays in the drawer as the most beautiful version of a page that shares whole, worth revisiting if the poster ever feels boxed-in. Whatever is picked: mockups here are the spec, and kitabi_screens.html Area 5 gets superseded by this file the day the pick lands.
